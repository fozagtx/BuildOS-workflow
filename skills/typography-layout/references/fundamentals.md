# Typography fundamentals

## Baseline

Horizontal reference line that letters sit on. Aligns words, numbers, and mixed faces.

**Baseline grid:** Repeated baselines at a fixed interval (e.g. every 20px). Use to align side-by-side paragraphs and keep vertical rhythm across a page.

## X-height

Height of lowercase letters (the height of `x`). Larger x-height → usually more legible at the same point size. Compare faces at identical size/leading/tracking before choosing a body face.

## Ascenders & descenders

- **Ascenders:** Parts rising above the x-height (`h`, `b`, `d`, `l`, …)
- **Descenders:** Parts dropping below the baseline (`g`, `j`, `p`, `q`, `y`, …)

Baseline is the reference for both. Tight leading can clip descenders or collide with the next line’s ascenders — watch multi-line display type.

## Letterform anatomy

| Part | Meaning | Design impact |
|------|---------|----------------|
| **Stroke** | Lines that build the letter (thin/thick, straight/curved) | Mood: rigid vs organic |
| **Serif** | Small finishing stroke at stroke ends | Tradition, formality, editorial |
| **Sans** | No serifs | Clean, modern, UI-friendly |
| **Counter** | Enclosed/open interior space (`o`, `e`, `p`, `d`) | Open counters → clearer reading; playful faces may vary counters for character |
| **Terminal** | Stroke ending shape (sharp, rounded, flared) | Personality: sharp = formal/harsh; round = playful |

Counters affect perceived boldness. Closing counters can make a word feel less sporty/bold even at the same weight.

## Kerning vs tracking

- **Kerning:** Adjust space between **specific pairs** so the word looks optically even.
- **Tracking (letter-spacing):** Uniform adjustment across a whole word/range.

Do not confuse them. Typical practice from the course:

- Tighten tracking slightly on bold display headlines for compactness
- Leave body tracking alone (or only tiny tweaks)
- Skip tracking tweaks on carefully drawn decorative faces

## Why typography owns UX

Typography drives:

1. **Readability** — Can users consume the content?
2. **Visual hierarchy** — What do they see first?
3. **Identity** — Does type match brand mood (playful, serious, historic, tech)?
