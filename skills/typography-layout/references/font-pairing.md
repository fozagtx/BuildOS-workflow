# Font pairing

Pairing is the highest-leverage creative move after basics. There is **no rigid matrix** of allowed combinations — any two well-chosen faces can work if roles and contrast are clear.

## Mindset

- Subject-area defaults (serif = fashion, bold sans = sports) are **recommendations**.
- Break the frame when the brief has a second story (e.g. tennis = athletic **and** historic/elegant).
- Software UI stays sans ~99% of the time — still leave room for a distinctive secondary if it serves brand.
- One excellent face can be enough. Pairing unlocks range; it is not mandatory.

## Role split

Always know who owns what:

| Role | Typical owner |
|------|----------------|
| Brand / hero words | Display, decorative, or characterful serif |
| UI / nav / body | Neutral sans or readable serif |
| Accents / quotes | Italic serif, script, or handwritten |
| Meta / captions / code-like | Mono or small caps sans |

Avoid two “main characters” at the same size and weight.

## Proven combination patterns (from course explorations)

### 1. Sans + Serif (classic)

- Sans as the system face (nav, body); serif for highlighted words or last line of a headline
- Or serif headline + sans body
- Tip: keep both in regular/medium so they do not fight; use size and case for drama
- Caps + selective serif letter swaps can add editorial spice

### 2. Display + Handwritten

- Heavy display + fluid handwritten looks “wrong” at first — strong in editorial, posters, image-led pages
- Split the headline across corners; vary word spacing for magazine energy
- Prefer low-copy contexts (photographer portfolio, campaign) if handwritten carries paragraphs

### 3. Sans + Decorative

- Decorative for big numbers, titles, project lists; sans for calm paragraphs
- Earthy or studio color systems support ornamented faces
- Bump paragraph size so the quiet face still reads next to loud ornament

### 4. Monospace + Serif

- Fashion / archival / 90s magazine vibe
- Serif for light compact headlines; mono for justified all-caps short blocks or article body
- Black-and-white often enough; add chapter numbers and meta for balance

### 5. Decorative + Serif (moody portfolio)

- Decorative headline + serif meta/body (often italic for article blocks)
- Overlap type on imagery only when contrast is solid
- Distribute small meta (date, AD, location) along a shared baseline for structure
- First-line indent on long serif paragraphs for editorial cue

### 6. Hybrid headline (identity blend)

Mix two faces **inside one headline** (word A in display, word B in serif/italic sans) to fuse two brand stories — e.g. sports energy + tradition.

## Progressive exploration method

For any brief, design three upgrades:

1. **Safe:** Expected face for the category (e.g. bold sports display + neutral sans)
2. **Shift:** Swap secondary toward the second story (e.g. introduce a traditional serif)
3. **Hybrid:** Mix both in the headline / key moments

Compare mood, not just “pretty.”

## Spacing & scale tips while pairing

- Play with **few** sizes (1–3) and paragraph scale — not five random sizes
- Uneven word spacing / wide gaps in short lines = editorial, not body text
- Movie-credit style split lines (start left, finish far right) only for **short** phrases
- Justified mono/serif blocks stay compact; left-align long reading if rivers appear
- Full-bleed image + type: protect contrast; never make users “puzzle” the words

## Anti-patterns

- Two high-contrast display faces competing
- Decorative/script as long form without a readable counterpart
- Same weight everywhere after pairing (hierarchy dies)
- Pairing that ignores the brief’s dual mood
- Over-tracking decorative faces “to make them fit”
