# Investigative Video Strategy

Reverse-engineered playbooks from 5 of YouTube's top investigative channels (Fern, Neo, Hoog, Cipher, Blackfiles). Use this skill whenever you're writing, planning, or critiquing a long-form documentary video.

## The one rule that overrides everything

Every video must:
1. Plant ONE macro question in the first 90 seconds
2. Refuse to answer it before the 85% mark
3. Use every other technique as a tool to keep the viewer engaged while they wait

If a draft violates this rule, fix that first. Everything else is secondary.

## Non-negotiables when applying these techniques

- **Specificity over abstraction.** Never write "a victim" — write "a 32-year-old software engineer there with his wife and son." Every channel ranks this rule highest.
- **Don't bolt twists on at the end.** Reveals must be the coherent explanation for anomalies planted earlier. If you reveal something the viewer wasn't quietly tracking, you've cheated them.
- **No manufactured tension.** Tension comes from dramatic irony, open loops, and moral stakes — not music cues or vague teases.
- **Curiosity, not exposition.** The viewer should feel like they're discovering, investigating, or solving — never being lectured.

## When the user names a channel

If they say "Fern style," "Hoog style," etc., open that channel's playbook first. Each channel has a *signature move* that defines it — lead with that move when emulating the channel.

## Stop before writing if any of these are unclear

- The macro question the video is delivering an answer to
- The specific named human (or composite) the viewer will follow
- What the viewer knows that the characters don't (dramatic irony layer)
- Where the 85% reveal lands and what it recontextualises

These four answers drive every other decision in the script.
