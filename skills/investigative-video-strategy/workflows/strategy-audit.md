# Workflow: Strategy Audit

Use this when the user gives you an existing script, outline, or draft and wants it audited for retention weak points.

## Step 1 — Read the whole thing first

Do not start critiquing until you've read the full draft. Most retention problems are structural, not local.

While reading, mark (mentally or in notes):
- Where is the macro question planted? Is it explicit?
- Where does it get answered? At what % of runtime?
- Who is the named human? Are they reintroduced consistently?
- What anomaly in Act 1 is supposed to be explained at the end?
- What does the viewer know that the characters don't?

## Step 2 — Run the diagnostic checklist

Score each item: ✅ present and strong / ⚠️ present but weak / ❌ missing.

**The four foundations**
- [ ] Macro question planted in first 90 seconds, explicitly
- [ ] Macro question NOT answered before 85% mark
- [ ] Named human (or composite) anchors abstract content
- [ ] Dramatic irony layer active (viewer knows something characters don't)

**Hook (first 90 seconds)**
- [ ] Uses one specific opener technique (cold open rewind / paradox / framing / irony / anomaly)
- [ ] No throat-clearing exposition before the hook lands
- [ ] Stakes are visible, not just implied

**Middle (90s → 85%)**
- [ ] At least 2 mid-loop techniques layered (see TECHNIQUES.md Problem 2)
- [ ] Every section closes one loop and opens another
- [ ] No section is pure exposition without a human, consequence, or open loop
- [ ] Named specificity throughout (no "a victim," "an employee," "sources said")

**Payoff (85% → end)**
- [ ] Reveal answers the macro question
- [ ] Reveal is the coherent explanation for an Act 1 anomaly (not bolted on)
- [ ] False summit OR third-act recontextualisation present
- [ ] Final beat leaves an aftertaste (systemic, moral, or forward-looking)

## Step 3 — Identify the top 3 fixes

Most audits surface 10+ problems. The creator can't fix all of them, and most are downstream of 2–3 root issues.

Pick the 3 highest-leverage fixes. For each, output:

```
FIX #N — [one-line headline]
SEVERITY: [critical / important / nice-to-have]
WHERE: [section / timestamp / line reference]
WHAT'S WRONG: [specific diagnosis]
TECHNIQUE TO APPLY: [name + which playbook it's from]
SUGGESTED REWRITE: [2–4 sentences of the actual change, not a description]
```

Critical = the video will lose retention without this fix.
Important = the video will work but underperform.
Nice-to-have = polish.

## Step 4 — Flag the one structural risk

If the script has a fundamental structural problem — wrong story shape, no dramatic irony available, payoff doesn't connect to setup — flag it explicitly. Don't bury it inside line-edit feedback. Lead with it.

Example:
> **Structural risk:** The macro question ("Why did the company collapse?") is answered in the cold open. There's no engine for the next 25 minutes. Recommend reframing around "Why did regulators ignore three warnings?" — a question the cold open *raises* instead of answers.

## Step 5 — Don't rewrite the whole thing

The audit's job is to find the 3 fixes and explain them clearly. Do not deliver a rewritten script unless the user explicitly asks. Most creators want a diagnosis, not a replacement.

If the user wants line-by-line edits after the audit, switch to the script-writing workflow.
