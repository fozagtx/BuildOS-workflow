# Technique Index

All 17 techniques across the 5 channels, grouped by the problem they solve. When deciding which to deploy, identify the problem first, then pick from the menu.

---

## Problem 1 — Open the video (first 90 seconds)

The macro question must be planted here. Pick ONE opener technique.

### Cold open rewind (Neo)
Open at the moment of maximum tension — capture, crisis, consequence. No context. Then rewind to explain how it was reached. Viewer already knows something extraordinary happens; they stay to understand how.

### Paradox hook (Hoog)
Open with a contradiction that should not exist. "A city famous for X, hiding the exact opposite underneath." State the contradiction in the first 30 seconds, before any context. The entire video becomes the explanation.

### Framing device (Fern)
A fictional scenario — a character, a crisis, a ticking clock — runs parallel to the factual content from the very first frame. The viewer learns through the thriller, never despite it.

### Dramatic irony setup (Cipher)
Tell the viewer in the opening what the people in the story don't know yet. The threat, the countdown, the inevitable failure. Every scene the characters experience normally, the viewer experiences as a countdown.

### Anomaly plant (Blackfiles)
Plant the central mystery as an anomaly that doesn't make sense yet — not stated as a question, but as a fact that doesn't fit. The resolution doesn't arrive until the final act.

## Problem 2 — Hold the middle (90s through ~85%)

This is where most documentaries die. Pick 2–3 techniques and layer them.

### Parallel narrative (Neo)
Run a second, completely separate story alongside the main one. Same crisis, same failure mode, same stakes. Let it play out to its full conclusion, then return to the main story with the emotional weight multiplied. **Two storylines for the price of one.**

### Investigation chain (Neo)
Never give the viewer the answer — give them the next clue. Every section closes one question and opens another. The video feels like a detective case being solved in real time, not a documentary.

### Self-contained case study structure (Cipher)
Break the long video into distinct mini-documentaries, each with its own beginning, mechanism reveal, and resolution. Each case study closes with a payoff, then immediately raises stakes for the next.

### Human anchor (Cipher)
Every large-scale story — hijacking, cyberattack, geopolitical crisis — is filtered through ONE named person at its centre. Their decisions, psychology, specific situation carry the viewer through dense structural content. The character doesn't need to be sympathetic; they need to be specific.

### Roadblock cadence (Hoog)
Almost every chapter follows: here is a proposed solution, here is why it failed. A built-in micro-payoff loop. The viewer is watching problems get solved and solutions collapse in real time.

### Surrogate character (Fern)
Place a fictional viewer stand-in inside the story for information-dense topics. The character experiences the subject alongside the audience — discovering it, not explaining it. Removes the feeling of being taught.

### Open-loop curiosity architecture (Blackfiles)
Lay evidence across the runtime, each piece making the central anomaly *stranger* rather than clearer. The resolution doesn't arrive until the final act — and when it does, it reframes everything that came before.

### Moral escalation (Blackfiles)
Don't just raise external stakes — raise *ethical* stakes. Move from "interesting crime" to "troubling pattern" to "systemic horror." The viewer watches their own moral response change in real time.

### Composite human character (Hoog)
Every abstract policy, statistic, or systemic failure is filtered through one named person with a specific situation. Every number gets a face before it gets a figure.

### Named specificity (Fern)
Victims, subjects, characters are NEVER statistics. Always named, given a job title, and one personal detail. "A park visitor was killed" → "a 32-year-old software engineer, there with his wife and son." The more specific the detail, the more real the stakes.

### Institutional accountability narrative (Hoog)
The driving engine is not a person — it is a system failing in plain sight. Policy decisions, their consequences, and the people who made them are traced through a clear cause-and-effect chain. The viewer is watching a paper trail being followed in real time.

## Problem 3 — Deliver the payoff (85% → end)

The macro question gets answered here. The reveal must feel earned.

### Third-act recontextualisation (Fern)
At ~60–75%, the specific story connects to something universally larger. A niche historical event becomes a story about a global corporation. A local scandal becomes systemic government failure. This pivot retroactively justifies the entire video and elevates it from interesting to important.

### False summit (Blackfiles)
Present what appears to be the resolution — the win, the capture, the takedown. Then immediately reveal it only bought a matter of weeks. **Double payoff:** the second hits harder because the relief was real.

### Coherent reveal (Blackfiles)
The final reveal is not a twist bolted on at the end. It is the coherent explanation for every anomaly quietly planted across the runtime. When it lands, viewers feel like they solved it themselves.

### Systemic payoff (Hoog)
The payoff is not a resolution. It is the revelation that the failure was not accidental — and that the same conditions still exist today. Leaves the viewer angrier and more aware than when they started.

## How to combine techniques

**Channel signatures (use as defaults if user wants a specific style):**
- Neo → cold open rewind + parallel narrative + investigation chain
- Cipher → dramatic irony + self-contained case studies + human anchor
- Blackfiles → anomaly plant + open-loop architecture + moral escalation + false summit + coherent reveal
- Fern → framing device + surrogate character + named specificity + third-act recontextualisation
- Hoog → paradox hook + roadblock cadence + composite human + institutional accountability + systemic payoff

**Universal stack (always present, regardless of channel):**
- One opener technique (Problem 1)
- The macro question, planted within 90 seconds
- Named specificity throughout
- Resolution at 85%+ mark that recontextualises what came before
