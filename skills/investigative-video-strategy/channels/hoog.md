# Hoog — Channel Playbook

## Signature move

**The composite human character.** Every abstract policy, statistic, or systemic failure is filtered through ONE named person with a specific situation. Every number is given a face before it is given a figure. The viewer is never asked to care about a problem in the abstract — they meet the person living inside it first.

Paired with this: **an exceptionally tight roadblock cadence.** Almost every chapter follows the same pattern: here is a proposed solution, here is why it failed. A built-in micro-payoff loop that prevents the video from ever becoming a lecture. The viewer is not being taught. They are watching problems get solved and solutions collapse in real time.

## Full move-set

### 1. Composite human character (signature)
- Build a representative human — real or composite — who embodies the systemic issue
- Give them a name, an age, a job, a specific situation
- Return to them whenever the systemic content becomes dense
- They are the proxy through which the viewer experiences the policy / failure / pattern

### 2. Roadblock cadence
- Each chapter introduces a proposed solution, then dismantles it
- "X tried to solve this by doing Y. Here's why it didn't work."
- The pattern itself becomes a retention engine — the viewer knows another attempt is coming, each more sophisticated than the last
- Each roadblock is a closed loop with a clear payoff (the failure mode); each one immediately opens the next loop (what gets tried next)

### 3. Paradox hook
- Every video opens with a contradiction that should not exist
- A city famous for one thing, hiding the exact opposite underneath
- A technology celebrated as a success, built on a foundation of failure
- The contradiction is stated in the first 30 seconds, before any context is given
- The entire video is the explanation for how that contradiction came to exist — and the viewer stays because they cannot leave without understanding it

### 4. Institutional accountability narrative
- The video's driving engine is not a person — it is a system failing in plain sight
- Policy decisions, their consequences, and the people who made them are traced through a clear cause-and-effect chain
- The viewer is not watching history. They are watching a paper trail being followed in real time.
- The payoff is not a resolution. It is the revelation that the failure was not accidental — and that the same conditions still exist today.

## When to use Hoog signature

- Policy failures (housing, healthcare, urban planning, regulation)
- Infrastructure stories (transit, energy, utilities)
- Systemic stories where the villain is a structure, not a person
- Topics where the audience needs to feel angry by the end, not entertained

## Voice and pacing

- Confident. The narrator is making a case, not asking questions.
- Statistics are always tied to the composite human within the same paragraph — never standalone
- Roadblocks are delivered with rhythm: setup, attempt, failure, "but then…"
- The closing beat is forward-looking, not resolved — it leaves the viewer with the conditions still in place

## Common failure modes

- **Composite human disappears in dense sections.** Fix: return to them at the end of every chapter. Use their experience as the punctuation.
- **Roadblock cadence becomes mechanical.** Fix: vary the *kind* of failure — political, technical, economic, social. Don't let three roadblocks in a row fail for the same reason.
- **Paradox hook is too abstract.** Fix: the contradiction must be visualisable in one sentence. If you can't picture the contradiction, the hook isn't working.
- **The ending tries to resolve.** Fix: this is not a resolution channel. The payoff is the revelation that the system still produces this failure — and now the viewer can't unsee it.

## Channel-specific signature checklist

- [ ] Opens with a paradox / contradiction in the first 30 seconds
- [ ] A composite human anchors every section where systemic content appears
- [ ] At least 3 chapters follow the roadblock cadence (proposed solution → why it failed)
- [ ] The paper trail of decisions is traceable through the video — cause and effect, not vibes
- [ ] The closing beat establishes that the same conditions still exist today
