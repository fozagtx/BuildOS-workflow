# Signal Scores

Use for scoring whether a framing is a good problem or a feel-safe one.

## Score each 1-5

5 is strong. 1 is weak.

| Signal | Ask | 5 looks like | 1 looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fragmentation | How many partial tools or manual steps are stitched together? | Four tools, two chats, a spreadsheet, and a person as glue | One clean job already served by one product |
| Incentive misalignment | Does the person who feels the pain lack the power or budget to fix it? | Doer eats the cost; buyer or incumbent owns the lever | Same person feels it, can pay, and can change the process |
| Incumbent neglect | Why would a well-resourced incumbent leave this unfixed? | Ticket size too small, would cannibalize a cash cow, or sits across org lines | Incumbents are already racing to ship this |
| Cost bearer | Is the cost concentrated on one worker who cannot pass it on? | Loss, discount, or hours land on one person every cycle | Cost is socialized, theoretical, or "the market" |

## Reading the scores

- Three or more signals at 4-5: proceed to sharpening.
- Mixed 2-3s: the framing is probably still bundled. Split the symptom and rescore (E03, E23). Do not average.
- All 1-2s: reject as obvious / feel-safe. Say why. Do not sharpen into a build (E22).
- Incumbent neglect 1-2: incumbents are already racing. Do not invent a structural gap (E12).

## Do not score

- How exciting the solution is
- How easy it is to demo at a hackathon
- How clearly the user can pitch it
- How large the TAM slide would look

Those are solutioning tells. They make a builder feel safe. Drop them and score the four signals.
